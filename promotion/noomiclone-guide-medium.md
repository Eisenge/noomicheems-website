# NoomiClone Beginner's Guide (2026): Controls, Swing Timing, and Practice Tips

**A short route from a search tab to a clean release — no download, no account, just four keys and a physics engine.**

If you recently landed on NoomiClone, you probably saw a tiny gymnast hanging from a high bar, arching, tucking, and letting go at the wrong moment. It looks simple. Then you release too early, or too late, and the character flails into the mat. This guide collects the published controls and a practice routine that keeps every attempt useful.

## What is NoomiClone?

NoomiClone is a physics-based browser game by **jamesnorth**, published on itch.io. You control a gymnast on a single high bar. The loop is short: build a swing, change your body shape, time your release, and try to land with something that resembles control. No collecting, no levels, no scoreboard pressure — the challenge is entirely in timing and reading the physics.

The browser release runs with keyboard controls and no account. You can play it directly in the browser.

## The four controls

The published controls for the browser release are:

| Key | Action | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left arrow | Arch | Watch the character's position before adding another input. |
| Spacebar | Tuck | Compare the tucked position with an extended one. |
| Up arrow | Let go | Change release timing one attempt at a time. |
| R | Reset | Restart after an attempt. |

If keys do not respond: click inside the game area to focus it, and wait for the game to finish loading. The published instructions do not document a map selector, a re-grab key, or touchscreen equivalents, so this guide does not invent those controls.

## Reading the swing

The whole game is a pendulum with a person attached. When the gymnast swings down through the bottom, they are moving fastest. Arching and tucking change the body's moment of inertia — a tucked body rotates faster through the swing, an arched body extends the motion. The developer's page documents the inputs; how you combine them is the game.

Practical way to start: ignore the release for your first few attempts. Just arch and tuck on the way through, watch how the speed changes at the bottom of the swing, and build a mental model of the rhythm.

## The release is the game

Everything before the release is setup. The two variables that matter at the top of the bar are **angle** and **momentum**. Let go too early and you drop almost straight down. Too late and you overshoot, sometimes spectacularly.

Rule of thumb for practice: vary only the release timing between attempts. Keep the same approach swing, same tuck, and shift the moment of release slightly later or earlier. One change at a time makes the feedback readable. Reset with R and try again.

## A simple practice routine

1. **Warm-up:** three attempts with no release at all — arch and tuck through the bottom, get a feel for the rhythm.
2. **Fixed swing, moving release:** keep your inputs identical, change only the Up arrow timing. Compare the flight path.
3. **Fixed release, moving body shape:** release at the same moment, change when you tuck. Watch how the rotation changes.
4. **Reset between attempts:** R keeps each attempt independent so you can compare.

This is a practice suggestion, not a developer-confirmed high-score technique. This guide does not claim scores, tricks, or records that have not been tested.
